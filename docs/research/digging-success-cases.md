# Digging-Game Success Cases

Status: production research  
Updated: 2026-09-29

Purpose: identify why commercially or critically successful digging games sustain an extremely simple "go deeper" fantasy, and extract rules useful for **flesh-pit**.

Sales figures are marked **official** when announced by a developer/publisher. Other figures are explicitly marked **estimated**.

---

# 0. Executive finding

Successful digging games repeatedly converge on the same structure:

> **one extremely legible physical action → a capacity/time/risk limit → return → a clearly felt improvement → a visibly more interesting next layer**

The fantasy can be explained in seconds.

The main failure mode is also consistent:

> **when depth only increases numbers, digging becomes labor.**

For flesh-pit this means the stomach/toilet/mutation structure is already structurally strong. The production risk is not "will players understand why they go deeper?" The risk is whether:
- returning to the restroom becomes commute time,
- mutations become percentage upgrades,
- deeper anatomy behaves like recolored high-HP dirt,
- eating stops generating new decisions.

# 1. A Game About Digging A Hole

## Commercial signal

- Released 2025.
- Developer announced **over 1 million copies sold** by August 2025.
- Later official Steam news described **over 1.2 million players**.
- The game began as a small experimental digging feature removed from a larger survival game and continued as a side project.
- Extremely low-friction pitch: buy a house, dig the backyard, find treasure.

Sources:
- https://steamcommunity.com/app/3244220/allnews/
- https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/designed-in-just-a-few-weeks-this-usd5-indie-game-about-digging-a-hole-has-journeyed-to-the-centre-of-steams-top-sellers-chart/

## Why it worked

### A. The title is almost the entire tutorial

"A Game About Digging A Hole" communicates:
- verb
- objective
- tone
- scale

There is very little interpretation cost before the player imagines the game.

### B. The hole is visible proof of progress

Progress exists simultaneously as:
- deeper number
- larger physical hole
- more valuable material
- better equipment

The player does not need a quest log to know whether they advanced.

### C. Small capacity forces punctuation

Limited carrying/battery capacity prevents a single monotonous infinite excavation session.

Return creates punctuation:
- stop
- cash in
- improve
- re-enter

The return is valuable because it promises a better next trip.

### D. Major tools change feel

The strongest progression moments are not "+10%":
- shovel-like digging
- powered drilling
- larger/faster excavation

This gives new sensory feedback and changes the excavation rhythm.

### E. Low price + short scope matches the premise

The game does not pretend the one-joke fantasy must support a 30-hour survival game.

That alignment between:
- title
- price
- runtime
- complexity
is part of the product appeal.

## Failure / ceiling

The concept becomes thin if later depth does not add enough qualitative variation.

The lesson is not "add lots of systems."
It is:
> **spend the limited complexity budget on changing the central verb.**

## flesh-pit extraction

Our pitch can be comparably immediate:

> **Eat your way deeper into a gigantic living body. When your stomach fills, go back to the toilet and vomit. Eating mutates you.**

That is far stronger than describing lore first.

The hole-equivalent visible progress is:
- physical eaten tunnel
- depth/anatomy change
- player's changing body/tools
- restroom accumulating evidence of progress

Detailed study:
- [A Game About Digging A Hole](../references/a-game-about-digging-a-hole.md)

# 2. Digseum

## Commercial / reception signal

- Released December 2024.
- $2.99 list price.
- SteamDB currently shows roughly **8,000 reviews and ~97% positive**.
- SteamDB lists third-party owner estimates around **265k–386k**; these are estimates, not official sales.
- All-time Steam CCU was modest (~600), demonstrating that a short premium incremental can sell strongly without becoming a large live-game concurrency phenomenon.
- Many players finish in roughly 3 hours.

Sources:
- https://steamdb.info/app/3361470/charts/
- https://steamcommunity.com/app/3361470/positivereviews/
- https://steamcommunity.com/app/3361470/negativereviews/

## Why it worked

### A. It has a real ending

Players repeatedly praise that it does not demand weeks or months like many incremental games.

A compact incremental can deliver:
- acceleration
- absurd power growth
- completion
without infinite retention design.

This is extremely relevant to flesh-pit.

### B. Upgrades arrive rapidly

Early game has little dead air:
- dig
- find relic
- museum output increases
- buy upgrade
- unlock another mechanic

The player nearly always sees a reachable next threshold.

### C. Digging has a tiny pattern-recognition layer

Early excavation asks the player to infer relic shapes rather than merely hold a button.

This minor decision layer helps a trivial action remain an action rather than pure waiting.

### D. Theme gives the numbers a physical home

The museum means passive income is not merely "+5/sec."
The player sees the collection expand.

## Failure / ceiling

Negative reviews identify an important collapse:
- prestige currency becomes the only thing that matters,
- money becomes decorative,
- optimal play becomes repetitive clicking,
- digging strategy becomes irrelevant,
- late upgrades unlock too late to matter.

This is exactly the sort of failure flesh-pit must avoid.

## flesh-pit extraction

Do not create:
- mutation points that make the actual flesh irrelevant,
- one optimal spam action that dominates all tissue,
- late mutations that appear after the player no longer needs them.

A mutation must alter the next physical encounter with flesh.

**Important:** the game can be only a few hours long. That is not a weakness if the escalation curve actually finishes.

# 3. Keep Digging

## Commercial signal

- Released September 2025.
- Clear pitch: up to 8 players, dig to 1000 meters, sell, upgrade.
- SteamDB shows an all-time peak around **8.4k concurrent players**.
- Third-party owner estimates on SteamDB range roughly **246k–363k**.
- Review sentiment is materially weaker than Digseum/SteamWorld Dig, around the high-70s/low-80s depending on snapshot.

Sources:
- https://steamdb.info/app/3585800/charts/
- https://gamerevenuedata.com/games/keep-digging/
- https://steamcommunity.com/app/3585800/announcements/

## Why it worked

### A. The finish line is numeric and absurdly clear

**1000 meters.**

This gives every session a visible macro-goal without story requirements.

### B. Co-op multiplies a repetitive physical task

Digging has strong "parallel labor" qualities:
- players independently remove terrain
- discoveries are easily shared
- giant holes are funny social artifacts
- progression can be witnessed by the group

The base action does not need complicated cooperation rules to become entertaining socially.

### C. Simple verbs are extremely streamable

A viewer can understand:
- how deep the group is
- whether a tool is better
- whether a discovery is rare
within seconds.

This is excellent short-form/video material.

## Failure / warning

The game later announced a much larger 2.0 update after scope expansion, while player reviews are less enthusiastic than the cleanest genre successes.

The useful warning is:
> success does not mean every added system improved the original premise.

## flesh-pit extraction

We are currently making a **small solo game**, so do not chase co-op.

Steal the product lesson instead:
- show depth constantly
- make milestones legible
- let screenshots/clips explain progress without context

A flesh-pit clip should immediately read:
"this person has eaten absurdly deep into something alive."

# 4. Dome Keeper

## Commercial signal

- Released 2022.
- Developer Bippinbits began as a two-person core team.
- Public reports state **$1 million gross around launch**.
- Publisher/developer anniversary material reported **over 500,000 copies sold** by the first anniversary.
- Current Steam review volume exceeds 20k with roughly 92% positive.

Sources:
- https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=16286
- https://www.capsulecomputers.com.au/2023/10/celebrate-dome-keepers-one-year-anniversary-with-the-new-double-dome-update/
- https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-dome-keeper-focuses-on-systems-that-feed-into-one-another
- https://www.pcgamer.com/dome-keeper-review/

## Why it worked

Dome Keeper adds **one external clock** to mining:
- dig underground
- carry resources
- decide when to turn around
- return before the next attack
- spend resources
- defend
- dive again

The brilliance is not "tower defense + mining" by itself.

It is that the defense timer changes every mining decision.

### A. "One more resource" becomes risk

The player constantly asks:
- can I reach one more deposit?
- can I carry all of this back?
- did I dig too far from the route home?

The mine is a time-management space.

### B. Weight converts loot into movement friction

More resources slow the return.

Thus reward and danger are physically coupled.

### C. Digging pattern matters

Players create:
- shafts
- horizontal search lines
- shortcuts
- landmarks

A good tunnel is infrastructure for future trips.

### D. Systems feed each other

Mining improves defense.
Defense creates time for mining.
Upgrades alter both.

Nothing feels like an unrelated minigame pasted on top.

## flesh-pit extraction

Our stomach can perform the role of Dome Keeper's timer without adding combat.

The central tension can become:

> **How much farther do I eat before my stomach forces the return?**

Potentially:
- fullness changes movement,
- being nearly full makes a rare detour risky,
- deeper tunnels raise return cost,
- mutations reduce that friction in qualitatively different ways.

These are **prototype questions**, not canon yet.

The crucial lesson:
> one pressure system that modifies the digging decision is better than five survival meters.

Our pressure system is already stomach capacity.

# 5. SteamWorld Dig

## Commercial signal

- Original released 2013.
- Image & Form CEO later stated the first game sold **over 1 million copies across platforms**.
- Steam user reception remains around the mid-90% positive range.
- The developer published an unusually useful postmortem focused specifically on digging design.

Sources:
- https://nintendoeverything.com/first-steamworld-dig-has-sold-over-a-million-copies-sequel-sales-on-switch-much-higher-than-other-platforms/
- https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-the-digging-mechanic-in-i-steamworld-dig-i-/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/252410/SteamWorld_Dig/

## Why it worked

### A. Digging is navigation, not only extraction

Every removed tile becomes permanent geometry.

The player simultaneously designs:
- descent
- future ascent
- platforming route
- resource access

### B. The designers softened punishment after playtesting

The original design intentionally risked trapping players.

Playtests showed many players:
- dug straight down,
- got stuck,
- failed to return with resources,
- stopped having fun.

The team first tried tutorialization.
It did not solve the problem.

They then added mobility such as wall jumping.

This produced a crucial design principle:

> The goal was not actually to trap the player. The goal was to make the player **feel wary of becoming trapped**.

This distinction is extremely valuable.

### C. Constraints are readable

Tile size, jump height, and movement rules directly determine viable tunnel shapes.

The player can learn the spatial grammar without a huge UI.

### D. Return friction is deliberately mitigated

Warp points/teleport solutions prevent the later game from degenerating into long climbs through already-solved space.

## flesh-pit extraction

Do not fetishize punishment.

Blood vessels, nerve contraction, or bad tunnels should create:
- tension
- improvisation
- recoverable mistakes

not:
- frequent unwinnable softlocks
- long forced resets

Likewise, when the player reaches serious depth, **restroom return distance will become a design problem**.

Plan eventual answers:
- anatomical shortcuts
- old service lifts
- mutations
- one-way return systems
- checkpoint restrooms

Do not decide which yet, but do not pretend the commute will solve itself.

# 6. Wall World

## Commercial signal

- Released 2023.
- Alawar publicly celebrated **200,000 copies sold** around two months after release.
- Later third-party estimates place Steam ownership substantially higher.
- Strong user-review volume and a sequel followed.

Sources:
- https://alawar.com/games/wall-world
- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/maria-urbanovich_indiegame-gamedev-activity-7069908846479069184-p8Mg
- https://steamrev.com/games/wall-world

## Why it worked

Wall World combines:
- mining
- mobile base
- timed monster raids
- run-based upgrades
- metaprogression
- biome discovery

It inherits a similar push-pull structure to Dome Keeper.

### A. Mine, but remember the outside world

The next attack gives every underground detour an opportunity cost.

### B. Biomes promise novelty

The world signals that upward/downward movement will expose:
- different visuals
- materials
- threats
- technologies

Depth is not merely a harder rock stat.

### C. Permanent upgrades make failure productive

Runs that end still accelerate later runs.

## Failure / warning

Player criticism repeatedly points to:
- interruption becoming annoying,
- returning to the mech taking too long,
- RNG determining key tools,
- later zones differing mainly by block durability,
- upgrades collapsing into a few dominant choices.

Again the same genre failure appears:

> **numerical escalation eventually replaces new excavation behavior.**

## flesh-pit extraction

Do not use random mutations to hide a thin progression tree.

The player should have dependable access to the mutations/tools required to understand a new tissue.

Randomness can modify a run later, but core tissue literacy should not depend on luck.

# 7. Motherload

## Role

Motherload is less useful as a modern commercial benchmark because clean comparable sales data is unavailable, but it is historically important as an ancestor of the loop:

**dig → cargo/fuel limit → surface → sell → upgrade → deeper**

The basic structure already worked in Flash-era browser play.

Sources:
- surviving/review retrospectives such as https://www.leetdom.com/reviews/motherload
- sequel reviews: https://www.gameinformer.com/games/super_motherload/b/playstation4/archive/2013/11/22/digging-deep-shallow-play

## Why the structure survived

Early upgrades change:
- reachable depth
- survivability
- fuel margin
- carrying capacity

Thus an upgrade answers a frustration the player experienced on the immediately previous trip.

## Failure / warning

Super Motherload reviews repeatedly complain that:
- repeated ascent becomes tedious,
- later progression changes too little,
- richer minerals deeper down can invalidate optional puzzle rewards.

This creates another durable rule:

> **Do not place a clever side activity in competition with a much more efficient "just go deeper" strategy unless the side activity rewards something unique.**

## flesh-pit extraction

A tumor, nerve route, optional organ pocket, etc. cannot merely give "more mutation points" if eating straight downward gives equivalent points faster.

Optional biological structures need unique consequences:
- mutation type
- new verb
- shortcut
- permanent world change
- rare visual/event

# 8. Core Keeper — useful success case, wrong scope target

## Commercial signal

- Early Access launch March 2022.
- Publisher announced **100,000 copies in two days**.
- **500,000 within two weeks**.
- **1 million within roughly the first few months**.
- It later grew far beyond our intended production scale.

Sources:
- https://fireshinegames.co.uk/core-keeper-sells-over-100000-copies-in-its-opening-2-days/
- https://fireshinegames.co.uk/core-keeper-hits-one-million-sales/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1621690/Core_Keeper/

## Why it matters anyway

Core Keeper demonstrates how mining progression can make new zones self-explanatory:

**new biome → harder walls → new ore → new workbench/equipment → access to next capability**

Each biome also introduces:
- enemies
- environmental hazards
- visual identity
- resources

The material tier and world tier reinforce each other.

## flesh-pit extraction

Do **not** copy its production footprint:
- crafting
- farming
- bosses
- multiplayer
- base building

Copy one thing:

> A depth zone should have a recognizable **material identity + mechanical identity + progression reward**.

For example, a vascular region should not merely look vascular.
Its entire loop should interact with bleeding, visibility, precision, and the mutation/tool obtained there.

# 9. Cross-case patterns

## Pattern 1 — the fantasy can be explained with one verb

Successful examples:
- dig a hole
- dig and defend
- dig for museum relics
- dig to 1000m

flesh-pit:
- **eat the giant body**

The vomiting/mutation twist is the second sentence, not the first.

---

## Pattern 2 — capacity is pacing

Backpack, fuel, weight, timer, raid cycle all answer:
**when should this expedition stop?**

Our answer:
**when the stomach fills.**

Therefore stomach capacity is one of the most dangerous balance values in the project.

It controls:
- excursion length
- return frequency
- mutation frequency
- restroom exposure
- perceived pace

Treat it like a director, not an inventory number.

---

## Pattern 3 — return must pay immediately

The successful cycle is not:

dig → commute → menu → commute → dig

It is:

dig → **cash-out/reveal/transformation** → stronger next dig

For flesh-pit every early restroom visit should do at least one meaningful thing:
- first vomit establishes reset
- mutation system becomes visible
- body changes
- tool appears
- environment changes
- shortcut unlocks
- new information appears

Not necessarily all at once.

---

## Pattern 4 — the central verb must mutate

Every case that receives repetition complaints eventually reduces to:
**same action, larger number.**

flesh-pit must escalate:

- grab/tear
- bite
- cut
- hook/pull
- saw
- suction
- cauterize
- possibly biological mutations that replace tools

Not all are required. The rule is **verb change**, not tool count.

---

## Pattern 5 — deeper must mean stranger, not tougher

Depth should change some combination of:
- geometry
- tissue behavior
- navigation
- information
- risk
- sound
- organism reaction
- tool preference

At least one meaningful new rule per major depth band.

---

## Pattern 6 — returning through solved territory is poison

Motherload, Super Motherload, Wall World, and SteamWorld Dig all expose this problem in different ways.

The farther the player gets, the more expensive the return trip becomes.

Eventually flesh-pit needs escalation such as:
- shortcut
- checkpoint restroom
- service infrastructure
- traversal mutation
- rapid return mechanic

The exact solution is not canon yet.

---

## Pattern 7 — visible macro-progress is disproportionately valuable

Examples:
- hole depth
- 1000m target
- biome changes
- museum growth
- upgraded machine
- rising wall position

flesh-pit should expose at least two simultaneous progress displays:
1. **depth/anatomical position**
2. **player mutation / excavation capability**

The physical tunnel itself supplies a third.

---

## Pattern 8 — optional detours need unique rewards

If "go straight down" always gives the best currency/hour, players will flatten every clever biological system into optimal tunneling.

Optional structures should offer things raw depth cannot:
- unique mutation
- tool
- shortcut
- information
- permanent modifier
- rare encounter

---

## Pattern 9 — small scope is compatible with major success

Especially relevant:
- AGADAH: tiny premise, >1M official copies
- Digseum: ~3-hour incremental, thousands of reviews and hundreds-of-thousands estimated owners
- Dome Keeper: began with a two-person core team and game-jam lineage

The opportunity is not "make a lot of game cheaply."

It is:
> **find one loop whose escalation curve can actually be completed within a small game's runtime.**

---

# 10. Recommended structural target for flesh-pit

Based on these cases, current best-fit structure is:

### Phase 0 — immediate fantasy
- player can tear/eat almost instantly
- no lore barrier
- first tunnel visibly persists

### Phase 1 — first forced return
- stomach fills quickly
- toilet/vomit is learned physically
- return trip is short

### Phase 2 — first transformation
- mutation points become apparent
- first upgrade creates obvious change, not +5%

### Phase 3 — first tissue rule
- blood vessel / nerve / valuable growth introduces a decision
- player realizes the mine is alive mechanically

### Phase 4 — first verb replacement
- hands are no longer sufficient or optimal
- new tool/mutation changes excavation feel

### Phase 5 — first major anatomical transition
- geometry/material/sound changes together
- depth produces a place, not a stat tier

### Phase 6 onward
Repeat:
**new tissue problem → new capability → old problem becomes easier → deeper tissue creates new problem**

Do not repeat:
**+HP wall → +damage upgrade → +HP wall**

---

# 11. Metrics worth prototyping

Do not balance from intuition alone.

Measure:

- seconds until first flesh removal
- seconds until first full stomach
- time from deepest point back to restroom
- percentage of cycle spent eating vs walking
- number of cycles between meaningful upgrades
- time until first new excavation verb
- time until first new tissue behavior
- average number of decisions per excavation minute
- how often players accidentally softlock their tunnel
- how often players choose optional tissue over straight descent

Especially important ratio:

> **return travel time / active eating time**

If this grows too large, the toilet loop becomes punishment rather than rhythm.

---

# Bottom line

The market precedent is strong enough that we do not need to prove "people like digging."

The problem to solve is narrower:

> **Can eating a living landscape generate the same readable progression satisfaction while producing more mechanical variation than ordinary dirt?**

The success cases say yes **if** stomach pacing stays tight, restroom returns transform something, and each deeper biological layer changes the way the player excavates.
