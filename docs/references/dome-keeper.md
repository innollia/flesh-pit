# Reference: Dome Keeper

Source role: **best reference for turning simple digging into dense micro-decisions with one external pressure**

- Game Developer interview: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-dome-keeper-focuses-on-systems-that-feed-into-one-another
- Raw Fury project page: https://rawfury.com/games/dome-keeper/
- Raw Fury original announcement: https://rawfury.com/announcing-dome-keeper/

## Success signal

Dome Keeper began as a Ludum Dare game-jam project by René Habermann and Anne Hecker, then became a commercial Raw Fury release.

As of 2026 the Steam store shows roughly:
- 19,000 Steam-purchaser reviews across all languages
- 93% positive among roughly 11,600 English reviews

Again, review counts are not direct sales figures, but this is clearly a large commercial/reception success for a project rooted in a two-person jam game.

## 1. The mining mechanic is intentionally simple

Director René Habermann says they wanted mechanics to be as simple as possible.

The complexity comes from:
- interactions
- interdependencies
- limited time

Mining itself remains mechanically legible.

### flesh-pit lesson

Do not make hand-eating mechanically complicated just because flesh permits complexity.

The base verb can stay:
- point
- tear/eat

Interesting play can emerge from:
- stomach limit
- route length
- vessels
- nerves
- valuable growths
- tool choice

Simple input, dense consequences.

---

## 2. External pressure creates hundreds of micro-decisions

Dome Keeper adds an approaching enemy wave.

Because the player must return before the attack:
- which direction to mine matters
- which ore to prioritize matters
- how much to carry matters
- whether to go one room farther matters

The developer explicitly describes mining as containing hundreds of micro-decisions because of this constraint.

### Important distinction for flesh-pit

We do **not** need enemy waves.

We already have a native pressure:
**stomach capacity.**

As the stomach approaches full:
- take the short route or risk another bite?
- eat low-value soft tissue for progress or detour to mutation-rich tissue?
- cut a vessel to shorten return distance?
- preserve a growth or destroy it for speed?

The stomach should do the work Dome Keeper's timer does.

---

## 3. Two systems must feed each other

Habermann's central design philosophy:
- battle gives a reason to mine
- mining produces upgrades that immediately matter in battle
- the phases create emotional contrast

The value is not "two game modes."
It is **mutual reinforcement**.

### flesh-pit equivalent

Our two spaces:
- flesh excavation
- restroom/mutation

They must feed each other.

Excavation produces:
- stomach fullness
- mutation accumulation
- discoveries

Restroom produces:
- reset
- mutation realization
- new body/tool capability
- anticipation for the next excursion

If the restroom becomes unrelated minigames, the loop breaks.

---

## 4. Strong upgrades, not timid percentages

Habermann says upgrades were intended to feel powerful, with big jumps the player can immediately feel.

### flesh-pit rule

Major mutations should be obvious within seconds.

Good:
- stomach capacity visibly doubles
- hand tearing becomes hooked ripping
- player can cauterize vessels
- new sense reveals nerves through flesh
- bone becomes cuttable

Weak:
- +3% eating speed
- +4% movement
- +2% mutation gain

Minor numbers can exist underneath, but milestone mutations must change play.

---

## 5. Gadgets delete old chores

Dome Keeper's lift is an especially useful example:
- early game requires manually carrying resources
- later the lift removes that recurring task
- progression therefore changes the loop instead of simply speeding it up

### flesh-pit equivalent

A late upgrade can remove an early friction:
- shortcut to restroom
- faster emptying/vomiting
- automatic blood clearing
- easier ascent through old tunnel
- local tissue suppression preventing contraction

This lets new complexity replace old complexity.

Do not make the player perform every early chore forever.

---

## 6. Emotional contrast matters

Dome Keeper deliberately contrasts:
- calm, curious mining
- tense combat

This keeps repetition from feeling flat.

### flesh-pit opportunity

Our contrast can be stranger:
- obsessive/gross eating in the pit
- banal quiet restroom reset

The restroom should not necessarily be scary every visit.
Its normality can make the flesh stranger.

Then depth milestones can disturb that normality.

---

## 7. Jam-origin lesson

The original concept came from a three-day Ludum Dare prototype around the theme "deeper and deeper."

The commercial version stayed recognizably close to that original loop.

### flesh-pit production lesson

The project should be prototypeable at game-jam scale:

Minimum fun test:
- one flesh volume
- hand eating
- stomach meter
- one restroom
- vomit/reset
- one mutation
- one hazard

If that is not compelling, more lore and content are irrelevant.

---

## One-line application to flesh-pit

> **Use stomach capacity as the clock that turns one stupidly simple action—eating a wall—into dozens of tiny route and greed decisions.**
